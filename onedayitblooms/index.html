One Day It Blooms A calm game about growing one rose

You plant a rose in mid-April and look after it the way gardeners do. Water it when the soil is dry. Cut faded flowers just above a leaf. Cover it before the hard frosts. It grows on its own schedule, and it keeps growing while your phone is in your pocket.

Is it for you?

You'll probably like it if

  • you grow roses, or you've always wanted to
  • you like a game you can open for five minutes and put away
  • you'd rather watch something grow than race a timer
  • rain on the window and bees in the garden sound like a good evening

Probably not, if

  • you want to build a farm, a town or an empire
  • you need something new to happen every minute
  • you want a hundred plants. Here it's one rose, looked after properly.

What you get

A few quiet minutes a day

Look at the soil, cut a faded bloom, check the forecast, and you're done. Miss a day and the garden carries on. The game lets you know when the rose really needs you.

A rose that reacts to what you do

Let the soil dry out and it wilts. Water it all the time and the roots suffer. Leave the faded flowers on and it sets hips instead of new buds. Feed it in time and the next round of flowers comes stronger.

Real rose care, not made-up rules

The cuts, the autumn mounding and the winter cover are the ones gardeners use on real hybrid tea roses. A bud needs a few days to open and a new shoot needs weeks to flower, same as outside. Garden days are just shorter than ours, so a whole year comes round much sooner.

A garden worth coming back to

Seasons change, the weather follows a five-day forecast, and there's always someone visiting: bees, butterflies, ladybirds, a bird at the feeder.

A year of rose care

The game starts in mid-April, when roses go into the ground. Each season asks for something different.

Spring March to May

Cut each cane above a bud that faces outward, so the bush stays open in the middle. Loosen the soil, add mulch, feed. New canes come up, then the first buds, and the aphids find them before you do. A ladybird house helps.

Summer June to August

Water at the roots when the soil is dry. Cut each faded flower just above a leaf with five leaflets, and a new shoot grows from that spot. Watch the leaves for black spot and mildew. After a storm, tidy any snapped cane with a clean cut.

Autumn September to November

The last flowers fade and hips start to form. Leaves turn yellow and drop. After the first frosts, mound soil around the base and shorten the canes for winter.

Winter December to February

Cover the rose once the frost has set in, not before, or it gets damp underneath. Air it out when it thaws. Mice go for the base of the bush. At the end of March, uncover it on a cloudy day and see how it did.

Whenever the ground isn't frozen, you can dig down to the roots: pick out grubs, cut away rot, and pull out the wild shoots that come up from below the graft.

The garden doesn't wait for you

Close the game and time keeps going. When you come back, you get a short summary of what happened: it rained twice, a bud opened, a bee came by. Autumn and winter pass faster, and slow down again when the rose needs a hand.

The climate is temperate: warm summers, real frosts and snow that stays on the ground. Check the forecast before you water. If rain is coming, it will do the job for you.

Bees and butterflies come to the open flowers. Ladybirds eat the aphids. A birdhouse brings birds that hunt aphids too, and a warm stone brings lizards that keep the ants away. In winter, watch out for mice.

Six roses to choose from

Each one has its own look, scent and habits. Pick by looks, or by the kind of gardener you are.

  • Honey Lantern

    Huge creamy yellow blooms with pink edges on a big, strong bush.

    Good at
    the biggest blooms, hardy, healthy leaves
    Watch out for
    always hungry, slow to bloom again

  • Peach Waltz

    Full, ruffled peach blooms with a strong fruity scent. Flowers in waves all summer.

    Good at
    the most buds, the strongest scent, more bees
    Watch out for
    thirsty, rain spoils the blooms

  • Cherry Brim

    Cream petals with a cherry red edge that deepens in the sun. Very fragrant.

    Good at
    a striking look and a rich scent
    Watch out for
    catches mildew and black spot easily

  • Lemon Arrow

    Bright lemon blooms on long, straight stems. Each flower lasts and lasts.

    Good at
    the longest-lasting blooms, shrugs off rain, fewer ants
    Watch out for
    fewer buds, hardly any scent

  • Ruby Goblet

    Velvety dark red blooms on long, straight stems, with a rich old-rose scent.

    Good at
    classic velvet red, rich scent, long stems
    Watch out for
    tender in hard frost, needs a good winter cover

  • Raspberry Spark

    Bright raspberry blooms with a sweet scent. New flowers come quickly all summer.

    Good at
    blooms again fastest, grows with energy
    Watch out for
    very prickly, mildew in warm, humid weather

When you just want to look

Cinema mode hides every button, and the camera slowly circles the garden. On a clear night you can look up at the stars. They turn slowly overhead, the way the real sky does.

There's a photo mode for your best bloom, and calm music with rain and birdsong underneath.

Good to know

Is it free?
Yes, and there are no ads. You earn Petals by looking after your rose and spend them on tools and supplies. Dew is the paid currency. It buys extras for the garden, like a bird feeder or a fountain, or opens a bud right away. You can grow your rose without it.
Do I need an account?
No. You can play as a guest. Signing in with Google Play Games is optional.
Does it work offline?
Yes. You can look after your rose offline, and it syncs the next time you're online. The shop needs a connection.
How much time does it take?
A few minutes, once or twice a day. The game sends a reminder when the rose needs you, and you can turn reminders off.
Which phones does it run on?
Android 7.0 or newer, on phones and tablets. You hold the phone upright.
Which languages?
English, Spanish, Portuguese, French, German, Italian, Russian, Japanese, Korean and Simplified Chinese.
Is there an iPhone version?
Not at the moment. One Day It Blooms is on Android only for now.
